The investment tax rebate (2026-27), explained

Assessment year 2026-27, for investments made 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026. Last checked 25 November 2026.

If you put money into DPS, Sanchaypatra, life insurance or other approved investments during the year, part of it comes off your income tax. This is the investment tax rebate. Here's how much you get, which investments count, and how much it's actually worth investing.

Work out your own rebate

How much you get

Your rebate is the lowest of three amounts:

LimitAmount
Based on your income3% of your taxable income
Based on your investment10% of your eligible investment
Fixed cap৳7,50,000

Taxable income here leaves out tax-free income, such as the tax-free third of salary and money sent home through banks. The rebate can bring your tax down to zero but not below it, and the minimum tax of ৳5,000 (৳1,000 for a first return) still applies.

How much is worth investing

For most people, the 3% of income limit is the one that decides the rebate. The investment limit only catches up when your eligible investment reaches 30% of your taxable income, because 10% of 30% is 3%.

Rule of thumb: eligible investment beyond 30% of your taxable income doesn't increase your rebate. With ৳8 lakh of taxable income, that's ৳2.4 lakh. You can still invest more, of course; it just won't cut your tax further.

Which investments count

Investments must be made during the income year, 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026. Some have a yearly limit on how much counts:

InvestmentHow much counts
DPS (deposit pension scheme)Up to ৳1,20,000 a year
Life insurance premiumUp to 10% of the sum assured
Sanchaypatra and other government securities, newly boughtUp to ৳5,00,000 a year
Mutual funds and unit certificatesUp to ৳5,00,000 a year (this limit is removed for investments from 1 July 2026)
Shares of listed companiesThe amount invested
Contributions to a recognised provident fundThe amount contributed
Donations to approved charities and fundsThe amount donated; some have their own limits

The yearly limits apply to the total of each kind, so splitting DPS across two banks doesn't raise its ৳1,20,000 limit. Keep certificates and statements for every investment; you enter the amounts in the e-return, and the tax office may ask for proof.

Sanchaypatra counts twice, in different ways. Buying new Sanchaypatra counts as an investment for the rebate. The profit you receive from Sanchaypatra is income, taxed at slab rates this year, with the tax already deducted credited back.

Worked examples

A salaried taxpayer with ৳8,00,000 taxable income

Their slab tax is ৳45,000 (see the tax rates guide). During the year they put ৳1,20,000 into DPS, bought ৳1,00,000 of Sanchaypatra, and paid a ৳30,000 life insurance premium on a ৳5,00,000 policy. All of it counts: ৳2,50,000.

3% of ৳8,00,000 income৳24,000
10% of ৳2,50,000 investment৳25,000
Fixed cap৳7,50,000
Rebate (the lowest)৳24,000

Their tax falls from ৳45,000 to ৳21,000. The income limit decided it: the last ৳10,000 of investment, above ৳2.4 lakh, made no difference.

Income ৳2 crore, investment ৳40 lakh (NBR's example)

3% of income is ৳6,00,000, and 10% of investment is ৳4,00,000. The rebate is ৳4,00,000: here the investment limit decides it.

Income ৳5 crore, investment ৳1 crore (NBR's example)

3% of income is ৳15,00,000, and 10% of investment is ৳10,00,000. Both are above the cap, so the rebate is ৳7,50,000.

In the e-return

You enter your investments on the e-return, and the portal works out the rebate and shows it as "Tax Rebate (On Investment)" on the Tax & Payment tab. Check it against the calculator. See how to file your return online for the full steps.
